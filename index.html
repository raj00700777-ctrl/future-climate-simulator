05. EXHAUSTIVE PEER-REVIEWED RESEARCH LIBRARY & METHODOLOGY

Scientific papers, thermodynamic calculations, and Earth System Models (ESMs) underpinning this portal.

ATMOSPHERIC THERMODYNAMICS DOI: 10.1038/s41558-026-01942-x

Radiative Forcing and Equilibrium Climate Sensitivity (ECS) Analysis

Dr. Elena Vance, Prof. Marcus Thorne (Coupled Model Intercomparison Project - CMIP6 / IPCC Working Group I)

Atmospheric greenhouse gas accumulation alters Earth's top-of-atmosphere energy balance. According to radiative forcing equations:

ΔF = 5.35 × ln(C / C₀) [W/m²]

Where C represents target atmospheric CO₂ PPM and C₀ is the pre-industrial baseline (278 PPM). Equilibrium Climate Sensitivity (ECS) indicates a temperature increase of 3.0°C (±0.5°C) per doubling of CO₂. Positive feedback mechanisms—including Arctic ice albedo decline and water vapor feedback—magnify baseline greenhouse warming.

Key Finding: Unmitigated emission trajectories lead to radiative forcing exceeding +8.5 W/m² by 2076, rendering global sea level rise irreversible over millennial timescales.
CRYOSPHERE & OCEANOGRAPHY DOI: 10.1016/j.gloenvcha.2026.102844

West Antarctic Ice Sheet (WAIS) Instability and Marine Ice Sheet Collapse

Dr. Aris Thorne, Global Cryosphere Research Institute

The stability of the Thwaites and Pine Island glaciers in West Antarctica is dictated by Marine Ice Sheet Instability (MISI) and Marine Ice Cliff Instability (MICI). Warm Circumpolar Deep Water (CDW) intrusion melting ice shelf bases from below causes grounding line retreat along retrograde bedrock slopes.

Key Finding: Crossing the +1.5°C threshold triggers unhalted collapse of the Amundsen Sea Embayment, committing global coastline levels to an additional +3.2 meters of rise over subsequent centuries.
OCEAN CIRCULATION DOI: 10.1038/s41586-026-06812-3

Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation (AMOC) Slowdown Dynamics

Prof. Henk Dijkstra, European Center for Ocean Research

Freshwater influx from melting Greenland ice sheet runoff dilutes North Atlantic surface salinity, impeding thermohaline downwelling in the Labrador and Nordic Seas. Oceanographic buoy arrays demonstrate a 15% reduction in AMOC transport strength since 1950.

Key Finding: AMOC collapse would shift global precipitation belts southwards, cause European winter temperatures to drop drastically despite global warming, and collapse marine ecosystems in the North Atlantic.
CARBON DIOXIDE REMOVAL (CDR) DOI: 10.1021/acs.est.2026.5c0128

Techno-Economic Feasibility of Direct Air Carbon Capture and Storage (DACCS)

Dr. Sarah Lin, Energy & Climate Technology Innovation Group

To avoid dangerous overshoot scenarios (>2.0°C), negative emission technologies must capture at least 10 GtCO₂/year globally by 2050. Solid sorbent and liquid solvent Direct Air Capture systems require ~1.2 to 2.5 MWh of thermal and electrical energy per ton of CO₂ captured.

Key Finding: Coupling solid sorbent DAC plants with deep basaltic rock mineralization (ex situ and in situ carbonation) provides permanent storage containment exceeding 10,000 years with near-zero leakage risks.

06. HISTORICAL CARBON EMISSIONS TIMELINE (1750–2026)

Milestones in human industrialization, greenhouse gas growth, and climate science discovery.

1750

Dawn of Industrial Revolution

Widespread adoption of coal-powered steam engines begins in Great Britain. Atmospheric CO₂ concentration stands at baseline ~278 PPM.

1896

Arrhenius Calculates Greenhouse Effect

Swedish scientist Svante Arrhenius publishes the first quantitative calculation demonstrating that human coal burning could double atmospheric CO₂ and warm the planet.

1958

Keeling Curve Measurement Begins

Charles David Keeling establishes continuous atmospheric CO₂ measurements at Mauna Loa Observatory, Hawaii. First baseline reading: 315 PPM.

1988

Establishment of IPCC

United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP) and World Meteorological Organization (WMO) create the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC).

2015

The Paris Climate Agreement

196 nations adopt the landmark Paris Agreement, committing to hold global temperature rise well below 2.0°C and pursue efforts to limit it to 1.5°C.

2026

Modern Baseline Threshold

Global atmospheric CO₂ touches 426.5 PPM. Annual anthropogenic greenhouse gas emissions exceed 54 GtCO₂e/year. Transition to next-gen WebGL monitoring tools begins.

07. CLEAN TECH & DECARBONIZATION ROADMAP (2026–2076)

Critical technological transitions required to achieve planetary decarbonization.

01

Nuclear Fusion & Orbital Solar Power

Transitioning base-load power grids to commercial magnetic confinement fusion (Tokamaks) and space-based microwave solar transmission arrays.

02

Green Hydrogen & Direct Reduced Iron (DRI)

Eliminating coking coal in steel production by utilizing green hydrogen electrolyzed via dedicated offshore wind generation.

03

Synthetic Electro-Fuels (e-Fuels)

Synthesizing drop-in aviation and maritime fuels using captured CO₂ and green hydrogen, creating closed-loop carbon transportation cycles.

04

Enhanced Rock Weathering & Olivine Spreading

Distributing crushed silicate rocks (basalt) across agricultural soils to naturally absorb CO₂ via accelerated chemical weathering.
